How quota studies cite evidence together

These selected passages concern India’s village council quotas. One sentence can credit several studies for investigating the same question; another can enlist them as evidence of the same result. Follow the wording to the cited evidence below.

Assignment

While the allocation of caste-based reservations is often based on population shares of these disadvantaged castes, female reservation is effectively random (Chattopadhyay and Duflo, 2004; Pathak and Macours, 2017).
Brown, Mitra & Singh (2025 working paper) · PDF p. 13

2 of 2 support it

Words assessed “female reservation is effectively random”

Narrower analytic reading. Both papers use rule-based assignment as a research design in the settings they study. Each invokes conditions under which the rule may be treated as random.

  • Chattopadhyay & Duflo (2004, Econometrica) · PDF p. 6
    Supports. “we could verify that the policy was strictly implemented”
    The authors reconstructed a serial-number rotation in their West Bengal district. Footnote 7 says they lacked the information to run the same check in Rajasthan.
  • Pathak & Macours (2017, Economic Development and Cultural Change) · PDF p. 7
    Supports. “conditional on these slight differences, assignment is random”
    Seats in Andhra Pradesh were rotated down a list ranked by population ratio. PDF p. 9 reports average female population shares of 0.501 versus 0.493 in the 1995 and 2006 groups, significantly different at 1%. The design argument is conditional.

0 of 2 support it

Words assessed “female reservation is effectively random”

Broader reading in context. Does the cited evidence establish as-if-random assignment across the districts and villages in Brown et al.’s sample? Brown et al. give their own balance check in the next sentence; this judgment concerns what the two references establish.

  • Chattopadhyay & Duflo (2004, Econometrica) · PDF p. 6
    Insufficient. “We could not obtain the necessary information to perform the same exercise in Rajasthan.”
    Checking the assignment roster in one West Bengal district does not verify assignment throughout Brown et al.’s separate sample.
  • Pathak & Macours (2017, Economic Development and Cultural Change) · PDF p. 9
    Insufficient. “(0.501 compared to 0.493)”
    The authors document a population-ratio difference in Andhra Pradesh and condition their design argument on this feature. Their data do not validate Brown et al.’s sample.

Jobs

Female leaders in village Panchayats also improve the quality and effectiveness of rural development programs, including the National Rural Employment Guarantee Scheme (NREGS) (Beaman et al., 2012; Deininger et al., 2015), and are better at providing local jobs under the NREGS as an insurance against negative shocks (Bose and Das, 2018; Deininger et al., 2020).
Brown, Mitra & Singh (2025 working paper) · PDF p. 6

1 of 2 support it

Words assessed “are better at providing local jobs under the NREGS”

Narrower analytic reading. Does research find more program employment received under reserved leadership? Actual work received can rise through demand, supply, or both. It does not identify a leader’s job allocation decisions by itself.

  • Bose & Das (2018, Review of Development Economics) · PDF p. 1
    Insufficient. “we find no difference between male and female leaders in generating employment”
    Demand rose, but the authors report no difference in employment generation. A null result supplies no affirmative evidence of an increase; it cannot establish an exactly zero effect.
  • Deininger, Nagarajan & Singh (2020, Journal of Comparative Economics) · PDF p. 8
    Supports. “increased the number of days worked by households holding job cards”
    Table 3 measures realized program days in eligible households. This supports the narrower reading about work received. The authors also document increased demand and awareness.

0 of 2 support it

Words assessed “are better at providing local jobs under the NREGS as an insurance against negative shocks”

Full clause, read as a claim of better protection during shocks. Evidence would compare the response to an adverse shock across leadership conditions. If the last words only describe the purpose of NREGS, the narrower reading applies.

  • Bose & Das (2018, Review of Development Economics) · PDF p. 1
    Insufficient. “generating employment or efficiently implementing public works under NREGA”
    The comparison is program performance by leader gender. It does not estimate whether employment cushions adverse shocks more effectively under female leaders.
  • Deininger, Nagarajan & Singh (2020, Journal of Comparative Economics) · PDF p. 8
    Insufficient. “a reservation-induced and persistent shift in the number of NREGS days women worked”
    An average change in employment can coexist with a larger, smaller, or unchanged response to shocks. The paper discusses the program’s insurance purpose but the leadership comparison here does not test that response.

Priorities

Our results on the effects of local female leadership are consistent with earlier findings that female politicians are more likely to focus on issues directly affecting women and girls, including health, education, and access to infrastructure (Chattopadhyay and Duflo, 2004; Beaman et al., 2012).
Brown, Mitra & Singh (2025 working paper) · PDF p. 22

Too vague

Words assessed “more likely to focus on issues directly affecting women and girls, including health, education, and access to infrastructure”

Does focus mean policy priorities, direct investments, or improved outcomes for girls? The sentence also leaves open which example belongs to which source. If these are complementary illustrations, each paper could contribute something different.

  • Chattopadhyay & Duflo (2004, Econometrica) · PDF p. 1
    Ambiguous. “leaders invest more in infrastructure that is directly relevant to the needs of their own genders”
    The paper supplies direct evidence on infrastructure priorities. The wording leaves open whether that one example fulfills this source’s assigned role or whether the source is asked to support the whole list.
  • Beaman et al. (2012, Science, PMC manuscript) · PDF p. 6
    Ambiguous. “their presence as positive role models for the younger generation that seems to underlie observed changes in aspirations and educational outcomes”
    Girls’ educational outcomes improved. The authors emphasize role modeling as an explanation. An effect of exposure to a leader does not establish that the leader focused policy on education.

Chair-identity

The literature on public good provision is concerned with how it is affected by population characteristics of jurisdictions (Banerjee, Iyer, and Somanathan (2005)), extent of democratization (Foster and Rosenzweig (2004)), and electoral outcomes (e.g., the group identity of the village-chair (Chattopadhyay and Duflo (2004); Gajwani and Zhang (2014)), her party affiliation (Dunning and Nilekani (2013)), geographic location (Besley, Pande and Rao (2012)) etc).
Das (2015 working paper) · PDF p. 6

2 of 2 support it

Words assessed “the group identity of the village-chair”

These two studies investigate how the gender of the council head relates to local public goods. The surrounding references concern different subjects. Das does not claim the estimated effects have the same sign.

  • Chattopadhyay & Duflo (2004, Econometrica) · PDF p. 1
    Supports. “the reservation of a council seat affects the types of public goods provided”
    The article compares public investment in councils with and without leadership reservations. It documents differing responses across goods and states.
  • Gajwani & Zhang (2014 online, 2015 WBER issue) · PDF p. 1
    Supports. “whether the gender of village government leaders influences the provision of village public goods”
    In Tamil Nadu the authors report fewer schools and roads built under women leaders. Das identifies the WBER article; it appeared online in 2014 and in the 2015 issue. A separate 2014 working paper has the same title.

Reservation-background

For more details about these well studied policies, we refer the reader to the literature (among others, Chattopadhyay & Duflo, 2004; Besley et al., 2004; Bardhan et al., 2010).
Cassan & Vandewalle (2021, World Development) · PDF p. 2

3 of 3 support it

Words assessed “more details about these well studied policies”

The preceding text describes reserving village council seats and presidencies for women and disadvantaged castes. As a pointer to institutional details, each source can cover a different part of that description.

  • Chattopadhyay & Duflo (2004, Econometrica) · PDF p. 6
    Supports. “every third GP starting with the first on the list is reserved for a woman Pradhan”
    The article explains the West Bengal allocation of council headships reserved for women.
  • Besley, Pande, Rahman & Rao (2004, author manuscript) · PDF p. 4
    Supports. “The choice of which GPs to reserve was left to individual states.”
    This source explains the rules for reserving headships for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes. The local author manuscript has different pagination from the published text. The same rule appears on printed p. 418 of the publisher version.
  • Bardhan, Mookherjee & Parra Torrado (2010, corrected JGD article) · PDF p. 8
    Supports. “Pradhan offices reserved for SC/ST are required to be proportionate to their demographic share”
    The corrected article explains the caste and gender reservations and West Bengal’s implementation. These three references supply complementary institutional details.

Water-infrastructure

For India, previous research has found that women leaders invest in public goods that are more in line with female preferences, in particular water infrastructure (27, 33).
Beaman et al. (2012, Science, PMC manuscript) · PDF p. 5

Beaman et al. (2012, Science, PMC manuscript) · PDF p. 7
“27. Chattophadhyay R, Duflo E. Women as policy makers: evidence from a randomized policy experiment in India.”

Beaman et al. (2012, Science, PMC manuscript) · PDF p. 8
“33. Beaman L, Duflo E, Pande R, Topalova P. Political reservation and substantive representation: evidence from Indian village councils.”

2 of 2 support it

Words assessed “in particular water infrastructure”

For drinking water specifically, do the cited studies find more infrastructure in councils reserved for women? This reading isolates the concrete example and leaves the general claim about all female preferences open.

  • Chattopadhyay & Duflo (2004, Econometrica) · PDF p. 23
    Supports. “In both places, there are significantly more investments in drinking water in GPs reserved for women.”
    In the West Bengal and Rajasthan districts studied, women also complained about water more often than men. Table V on PDF p. 24 reports counts of newly built or repaired water facilities.
  • Beaman et al. (2010–11, India Policy Forum) · PDF p. 18
    Supports. “significantly more public drinking water taps and hand pumps when the GP is reserved for a woman”
    The Millennial survey comparison adds evidence from multiple states (PDF pp. 6–7, 17–18). The paper also analyzes a 2005 survey in Birbhum (pp. 7, 21), a district studied in the 2004 paper. The two citations agree on water infrastructure and share an author and some geography.
